Every birthday, on time:
Grandma's journey to Birthday Pi

Grandma works hard to make every family member feel important and connected. This storyboard follows what happens when a birthday slips by, and proposes a simple at-home product that makes sure birthdays, anniversaries and milestones are never missed again.

Too little lead timeRemembering on the day is too late to mail a card or send a gift.

The journey

1

Meet Grandma

Proud, connected

Family is Grandma's whole world. She uses her smartphone for calls, texts and photos, and her photo wall keeps filling up with grandkids, great-grandkids and in-laws from a big, diverse family.

2

The paper calendar fails

Stretched, unsure

Every birthday lives on a paper calendar. Names spill into the margins and onto sticky notes, and when the new year's calendar goes up, not everyone makes it across. Nothing tells her who is missing.

3

The day arrives

Shocked

She finds out from party photos posted to the family chat on her phone. The birthday is already here.

4

Too late to mail

Stressed

She rushes to mail a card and a gift. With no lead time left, both will arrive days after the birthday.

5

A late call

Guilty, low

The phone call comes late too. Her love is real, but missing the day leaves her feeling she let someone down, and it wears at the connection she works so hard to keep.

6

Birthday Pi joins the family

Hopeful

Birthday Pi is a small Raspberry Pi on Grandma's home Wi-Fi. When family visits, they open it in a browser on their phones and add birthdays, anniversaries and milestones. Grandma can update any date from her phone or tablet and decides which milestones belong on the list. The monitor shows what's coming up, with photos of the people being celebrated. It can't be reached from outside her Wi-Fi.

7

A reminder she can't miss

Reassured

Two weeks out, a gift reminder shows up on her screen. It refreshes every day, and the text gets bigger and easier to read until she taps "Got it". One tap clears it on her phone, tablet and monitor. The card reminder comes one week out and the call reminder on the day. It's only a reminder, not a to-do list.

8

Celebrate on time

Joyful, connected

Birthday Pi takes away the hard parts and the mistakes, not the joy. Grandma still picks the gift, writes the card and makes the call, and now they arrive on time for birthdays, anniversaries and milestones.

Grandma's emotional arc

The designer's reading of how Grandma feels in each frame. The dip in frames 3 to 5, the missed and late birthday, is the moment the product has to prevent.

The proposed product: Birthday Pi

Birthday Pi is a small Raspberry Pi on Grandma's home Wi-Fi. She opens it in the browser on her phone, tablet or another smart screen, and it can also show on a monitor plugged into the Pi, which displays what's coming up, any active reminders and rotating photos of the people being celebrated. It reminds her early enough that the gift, card and call all arrive on time.

1. At home, on her Wi-Fi

Birthday Pi runs on Grandma's home network and works in any browser there. It can't be reached from outside her Wi-Fi.

2. Everyone keeps it up to date

When family visits, they add birthdays, anniversaries and milestones. Grandma can update any date herself, and she decides which milestones go on the list.

3. A reminder she can't miss

  • 2 weeks before: gift reminder
  • 1 week before: card reminder
  • On the day: call reminder

Birthdays, anniversaries and milestones all use the same timing. Each reminder refreshes daily, and its text gets bigger and easier to read until she taps "Got it." One tap clears it on every screen. It's only a reminder, not a task tracker.

4. Photos of who's coming up

A photo library shows pictures of the people with upcoming celebrations. Each photo fades in and out every 5 minutes.

5. Roadmap: integrations, not complication

Birthday Pi grows by connecting to other tools, not by adding steps. It takes away the hardship and the mistakes, never the joy of celebrating.

Open design questions for the next round

Your answers settled most of the earlier questions. These are still open.

  1. First integrations.Which integrations come first on the roadmap, and in what order?
  2. Adding photos.Who adds photos to the library, and how? Is it family during visits, Grandma, or both?
  3. Where photos show.Should the rotating photos appear only on the monitor, or on her phone and tablet too?
  4. What counts as "upcoming."How far ahead should a person's photos start rotating: two weeks, a month, or something else?
  5. Biggest text size.Should the reminder text stop growing at a maximum size, and what if two reminders are active on the same day?
